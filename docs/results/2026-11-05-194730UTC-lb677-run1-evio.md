# LB 677 run 1: EVIO time frames from NERSC to two receivers (2026-11-05 19:47:30–20:06:04 UTC)

The first run with data on LB 677, container `e2sar_lb677_1793905825` (run 1; [run 2](2026-11-05-201815UTC-lb677-run2-evio.md), `e2sar_lb677_1793909535`, followed at 20:18:15 UTC). It used the pipeline of [the LB 675 run](2026-11-04-nersc-lb675-evio.md) while the sender side set up the full 18-crate streaming readout (SRO); the frames of this run hold one ROC only (see [What is in the events](#what-is-in-the-events)). A frame builder at NERSC sent time frames back to JLab through an LB, to two receivers on the ebpf testbed writing one DAOS container, read live into `/scratch/epsdaq`. E2SAR 0.4.0rc1, pool `sc26`, `main` at 68d99f1. The sender side is not ours: its commands and settings are not known here.

This time the NIC and socket counters were sampled every second on both receivers, as the LB 675 result asked for. With them, and with the per-event writer slots in DAOS, the burst of missing events can be placed.

## Setup

LB 677 was reserved on the beta control plane (`ejfat-lb.es.net:18008`) with the admin token from the private `~/.bashrc`: `lbadm --reserve -l xmei-e2sar -d 168:00:00 -e > ~/e2sar-instance.env`, no registered senders. The instance URI was given to the sender side, whose senders registered their own addresses.

Both pages were forwarded to a laptop as in [live-monitoring](../howto/live-monitoring.md). The LB gave each receiver 256 of its 512 slots. Logs and counter files are kept on the nodes in `~/e2sar_run/lb677/<run>/`.

## Before data arrived

The pipeline waited 2 h 20 min in two containers (`e2sar_lb677_1793897219`, `e2sar_lb677_1793900903`) that got 0 events: no sender was registered on the LB, and no UDP packet reached the receivers. Both were destroyed. By 19:30 UTC sender 198.124.49.31 had registered, and by 19:49 also 128.55.83.219 (NERSC). Data began at 19:47:30 in the third container, `e2sar_lb677_1793905825`.

## What is in the events

Each event is one EVIO v6 record holding one time frame (bank `0xff60`). Decoding all 75,752 records of the 19:57:50 file:

- The frame number in the stream-info bank is always the E2SAR event number minus 1, and the frame timestamps step by exactly 65,536 ns per event number, across every gap too. So a missing event number is a missing frame, not a skipped number.
- The record number in the record header advances once per two events: odd event numbers carry one record sequence, even ones another (37,881 and 37,871 events, no exception). The frames come from two senders taking turns, each counting its own records. Which sequence belongs to which address isn't known.
- **One ROC per frame, not 18.** Every sampled frame has exactly one ROC bank, ROC id 12, with 13 payload banks (ports). Run 2 is the same (ROC 12 only, in its 20:36:05 file).

### Hit words vs all words

Counted with the layout of evio-interactive-view's `evio_decode.py` (a payload bank of length 1+N holds N fADC hit words; everything else is headers and stream info), on every 100th event of run 1 (170,271 frames from all 224 files):

| Part of a frame | Words per frame | Share of all words |
|---|---|---|
| Record header (no index array) | 14 | 0.6% |
| Frame bank `0xff60` header | 2 | 0.1% |
| Frame stream info `0xff31` (frame, timestamp, ROC list) | 8 | 0.3% |
| ROC bank header | 2 | 0.1% |
| ROC stream info `0xff30` | 14 | 0.6% |
| Payload bank headers (13 per frame) | 26 | 1.1% |
| **Hit words** | **2,303.3** | **97.2%** |
| **All words** | **2,369.3** (9,477 bytes) | 100% |

| Per frame | p50 | mean | p99 | min / max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All words | 2,369 | 2,369.3 | 2,725 | – / 3,014 |
| Hit words | 2,303 | 2,303.3 | 2,659 | 1,591 / 2,948 |
| Hit words / all words | 97.2% | | 97.6% (p1 96.7%) | |

The overhead is fixed at 66 words (264 bytes) per frame, so the hit share only moves with the hit count. 2.1% of the payload banks are empty (no hits on that port in that frame). In run 2's 20:36:05 file (every 20th event) the frames are a little busier: 2,595 hit words of 2,661, 97.5%. How far these frames compress, and whether Parquet helps when there are so few headers: [hit-compression](../concepts/hit-compression.md) (ideas, draft).

## The missing events: lost on the way to ebpf2201

All 536 missing events are between event numbers 9,581,228 and 9,582,383 (1,156 frames, ~76 ms, in the file of 19:57:50 UTC), as 280 gaps of 1 to 9 events. The other 223 files have no gaps.

**Not the receivers' NIC or sockets.** Around the burst (19:57:38 to 19:58:06), both nodes received 51,000 to 56,000 packets/s with no change in `rx_discards_phy`, `rx_out_of_buffer`, `RcvbufErrors` or `InErrors`. Over the whole run the NIC counted 60,439,545 packets on ebpf2201 and the UDP layer 60,391,327; on ebpf2203 60,805,826 and 60,364,600. The difference is other traffic on the port, of the size seen while the pipeline was idle, with no drop counter moving. So every packet that reached a receiver's NIC reached its socket.

**Whole events, from both senders.** 536 events vanished completely, while only one partial event was logged in the burst (receiver A's reassembly loss, in its 10 s report of 19:58:01). Random packet drops would leave many partial events at ~7 packets each. 271 of the missing events are even and 265 odd: both sender sequences lost frames at the same moment, so it isn't one sender's host.

**Only ebpf2201's share.** `e2sar_daos_read --list` gives the writer slot of each stored event. In the burst's event range, receiver B stored its usual half, 589 events, and receiver A only 31. Next to the burst (event numbers 9,580,000 to 9,584,000 outside it) the split is even: 1,411 and 1,434. Of the 1,156 frames in the range, receiver B stored 589 and receiver A 31; the other 536 are the missing ones. So nearly all of A's ~578 frames were lost, and none of B's. Receiver A's 10 s rate dipped at 19:58:01 by about the size of the burst.

So the frames were sent (both sequences, and the other receiver got its half), the LB split them as usual, and those for ebpf2201 did not reach its NIC for ~76 ms. That leaves the path from the LB to ebpf2201, or the LB's forwarding to that one worker. A gap in ebpf2201's packet rate can't show it: ~3,800 packets in one second is inside the ±2,000 packets/s jitter.

| | 19:27:55 | 525 | A (ebpf2201), from its rate dip |
| LB 677 run 1, `e2sar_lb677_1793905825` | **19:57:50** | 536 | A (ebpf2201), from the writer slots |
| [LB 677 run 2](2026-11-05-201815UTC-lb677-run2-evio.md), `e2sar_lb677_1793909535` | **20:27:50** | 584 | A (ebpf2201), from the writer slots (34 vs 608) |

Four of five bursts fall at :27:50–:27:55 or :57:50 UTC, every 30 minutes, three of those on ebpf2201, each ~525–584 events (~76 ms). Nothing on ebpf2201 runs then: its cron jobs are at 03:00, 07:00 and :01, and `sysstat-collect` every 10 minutes from :00. And its NIC dropped nothing. Something periodic upstream is the likely cause: in the LB, the network between the LB and the testbed, or a half-hourly event on the sender side that affects only one worker's traffic.

## Notes

- **The NIC sampler logged `NoPorts` as `InDgrams`** until 20:15 UTC: the third column of the `Udp:` line, not the second. The drop columns (`InErrors`, `RcvbufErrors`) were right all along. The before and after snapshots keep the whole `Udp:` line, which is where the `InDatagrams` counts above come from. Fixed during run 2.
- `e2sar_daos_read --list` on this run (17M events) takes over 2 minutes and printed one DAOS client error (`dc_rw_cb() ... RPC ... to 5/15`), then finished with every event listed.
- The data is kept: the container, the reader's files, the monitor file `/scratch/epsdaq/e2sar_lb677_1793905825_monitor.jsonl` and the logs.
